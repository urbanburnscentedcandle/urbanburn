Looking after your candle

Five habits that make a candle last longer, throw more scent, and burn safely.

  • Trim the wick to 5mm before every burn. An untrimmed wick burns tall, sooty and hot. Trimming is the single thing that most improves how a candle behaves.
  • Let the first burn form a full pool. Burn until melted wax reaches the edge of the vessel — usually two to three hours. Soy wax remembers its first burn, and cutting it short leaves a tunnel you can't undo.
  • Burn for no more than four hours at a time. Longer than that and the vessel gets hot and the wick starts to mushroom. Let it cool fully before relighting.
  • Keep it away from draughts, curtains, children and pets. Set it on a heat-resistant surface, and never leave a lit candle unattended.
  • Stop when about 10mm of wax remains. Below that the flame sits too close to the base of the vessel.

Cleaning out the empty vessel

Put the vessel in the freezer for three to four hours. The wax contracts and lifts out in one piece — press gently with a spoon if it needs help. Wipe out the residue, wash with warm soapy water, and pull the wick tab off with pliers.

Do not pour boiling water into heavy stone or concrete vessels. The thermal mass makes cracking a real risk, and the freezer method works better anyway.
